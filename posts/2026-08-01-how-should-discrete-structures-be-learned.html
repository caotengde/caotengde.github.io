How Should Discrete Structures Be Learned?

A position note on continual, executable, and verifiable structure learning.

Jump to citation ->

Large language model agents increasingly rely on skills, rules, programs, and explicit world models. These structures can be executed, inspected, and reused, allowing agent behavior to exist outside opaque neural weights. Yet they introduce a fundamental question that remains insufficiently addressed: How should discrete knowledge structures be trained?

Neural networks typically update continuous parameters within a fixed computational structure. Updating discrete knowledge may instead require adding a condition, removing a rule, changing a dependency, or rewriting part of a program. Such updates alter the model itself, not merely its parameters. A rewrite generated by a language model therefore cannot automatically be treated as a learning result. Successful and failed trajectories provide evidence, but they do not by themselves establish that a modification is correct.

Our position is:

Learning discrete structures should be understood as the continual revision of executable models, not as a textual analogue of gradient-based training.

A trustworthy update should satisfy at least three principles:

  1. Executability: Knowledge must affect behavior or predict outcomes rather than remain a natural-language description.
  2. Traceability: A modification should refer to identifiable components and dependencies instead of opaquely rewriting the entire knowledge structure.
  3. Verifiability: A proposed modification should become persistent knowledge only when independent checks show that it improves the intended objective without damaging established capabilities.

From this perspective, a skill is an important unit of execution, but it should not be treated as the only unit of knowledge. An agent must also represent states, relations, constraints, mechanisms, and dependencies among components. Whether these elements are implemented as graphs, rules, programs, or another formalism may depend on the task. What matters more is whether they can be executed, compared, revised, and verified.

This view also changes the role of reflection. A language model may help identify problems and propose modifications, but reflection alone is not training. A new structure becomes part of the learned model only after it has been made executable and checked against environmental evidence and regression tests.

The central question in discrete structure learning is therefore not how to generate more text or accumulate more skills, but:

Which structural changes deserve to become knowledge that an agent retains over time?

Answering this question requires executability, local revisability, and verification to be treated as parts of the same learning framework. This may be a necessary foundation for building agents whose knowledge remains maintainable, interpretable, and capable of evolving over long periods.

离散结构应当如何学习？

大语言模型智能体正在越来越多地使用技能、规则、程序和显式世界模型。这些结构能够被执行、检查和复用，也使智能体的行为不必完全隐藏在神经网络权重中。然而，它们带来了一个尚未得到充分回答的问题：离散知识结构应当如何训练？

神经网络通常在固定计算结构中更新连续参数。离散知识的变化却可能意味着增加一个条件、删除一条规则、调整模块依赖，或者修改一段程序。这样的变化不仅改变参数，也改变模型本身。因此，把语言模型生成的一次重写直接视为学习结果，是不充分的。成功或失败轨迹只能提供证据，不能自动保证修改正确。

我们的立场是：

离散结构学习应被理解为可执行模型的持续修订，而不是梯度训练的文字版本。

一个可信的更新至少应满足三个原则：

  1. 可执行：知识必须能够影响行为或预测结果，而不只是自然语言描述。
  2. 可追踪：修改应当对应明确的组件和依赖，避免整套知识被不透明地重写。
  3. 可验证：候选修改只有在独立检查中改善目标且不破坏已有能力时，才能成为持久知识。

从这个角度看，Skill 是重要的执行单元，但不应被视为唯一的知识单元。智能体还需要表示状态、关系、约束、机制和不同组件之间的依赖。具体采用图、规则、程序还是其他形式，可以由任务决定；更重要的是，这些表示能否被执行、比较、修订和验证。

这一观点也改变了我们对“反思”的理解。语言模型可以帮助发现问题和提出修改，但反思本身并不等于训练。只有当修改落实为可执行结构，并经过环境证据和回归检查，新的结构才真正成为模型的一部分。

因此，离散结构学习的关键问题不是如何产生更多文本或更多技能，而是：

什么样的结构变化，才有资格成为智能体长期保留的知识？

回答这个问题，需要把可执行性、局部可修改性和验证机制放在同一个学习框架中。这可能是构建可维护、可解释并能够长期演化的智能体所必须面对的基础问题。
